TRAINING LAB — TEACH A MODEL, LIVE

Inference predicts. Training learns. Run gradient descent on a small transformer run: forward pass → loss → backward pass → optimizer step, thousands of times. Your knobs decide whether it converges, crawls, or blows up.

ONE TRAINING STEP — WATCH IT CYCLE

1 · FORWARD PASS

A batch of text flows through the network. Every weight participates. Out come predictions for the next token at every position.

2 · LOSS

Predictions are scored against the real next tokens (cross-entropy). Loss ≈ 4.6 means "very wrong"; ≈ 1.0 means the model mostly knows what's coming.

3 · BACKWARD PASS

Backpropagation applies the chain rule layer by layer, computing how much each weight contributed to the error — its gradient.

4 · OPTIMIZER STEP

w ← w − lr × ∇w

Each weight nudges against its gradient. Too big a nudge (high lr) and training diverges. Too small and it takes forever.

HYPERPARAMETERS — WIRED TO THE RUN

The single most important knob in training. Past ~0.03 on this run, gradients explode and loss goes NaN. That's a real failure mode, not a bug.

Bigger batch = smoother, less noisy gradients and higher throughput — but each step costs more compute.

Each GPU trains on its own shard of the batch, then all GPUs average gradients in an NCCL all-reduce ring. More GPUs = faster steps, with growing sync overhead.

Lower precision = more throughput per watt. Production runs mix: BF16/FP8 math with FP32 master weights.

SIREN TUNER — SINUSOIDAL NETWORKS & THE FACILITY ALARM

SIREN networks use sin(ω₀x) activations instead of ReLU, so they can represent fine detail — and their derivative is another SIREN (a cosine). The same math shapes the alarm synth below.

ω₀ scales the input frequency. High ω₀ = fine detail, harder to train. The SIREN paper's default is 30.

Overtones summed into the waveform: Σ sin(k·ω₀x)/k. More harmonics = richer, more piercing alarm.

Audio starts only after you tap Trigger — browsers block autoplay, and the siren is a live Web Audio oscillator, not a recording. Sweep ω₀ and the carrier while it runs.

THE REAL NUMBERS — USE THESE, NOT HOLLYWOOD'S

GPU power (B200-class)≈ 1,000 W per GPU at full load. 128 GPUs ≈ 128 kW of IT load, ≈ 165–175 kW with CPUs, networking, and storage. If a sim shows ~11 kW per GPU, it's wrong by 10× — always sanity-check watts per GPU.
Throttle pointJunction (die) temperature throttles around 83–90 °C on H100/B200-class parts. ~52 °C figures you may see elsewhere are coolant or case temps — a different sensor entirely.
CoolingProduction AI halls use chilled water and direct-to-chip liquid cold plates. Liquid nitrogen is for overclocking records, not production. PUE 1.1–1.3 is realistic; 1.09 is best-in-class on a good day.
Bus mathThree-phase power: P = V × A × √3 × power factor. 170 kW at 480 V ≈ 230–260 A. Check any dashboard against this before trusting it.
Emergency Power OffEPO kills compute and cooling together. Temperatures coast up briefly on residual heat, then fall slowly. Anything that instantly freezes a rack to room temp is fiction.
InterconnectTraining clusters move gradients over InfiniBand / NVLink fabrics (400–800 Gbps per link). The all-reduce sync is why adding GPUs stops scaling linearly.

HOW EACH MODULE MAPS TO THE REAL THING

Machine Room is a first-order thermal model: each rack's junction temperature chases an equilibrium set by load, coolant temperature, and flow, with a time constant — which is why recovery takes sim-minutes, not a click. Training Lab runs a stylized but honest gradient-descent loop: loss really does fall exponentially-ish with steps, really does explode past a learning-rate cliff, and checkpoints really are the only thing standing between you and restarting a multi-day run. Agent Loop is the ReAct pattern used by real agent frameworks, with the part demos usually hide: tools fail, and the loop has to notice. Next-Word is production sampling math, unmodified. SIREN is the actual activation and derivative from the Sitzmann et al. paper, driving a real oscillator.

Play order if you're new: Next-Word (5 min) → Training Lab (Mission 3) → Machine Room (Missions 1–2) → Agent Loop (Mission 4) → SIREN whenever your ears are ready.
